# dInfer: An Efficient Inference Framework for Diffusion Language Models

- authors: 23 authors (Yuxin Ma, Lun Du, Lanning Wei, Kun Chen, et al.)
- venue: arXiv preprint
- year: 2025 (submitted 2025-10-09)
- url: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08666
- tags: [dllm-cache, inference-framework, vicinity-refresh, kernel-engineering]

## Key claims

- Inference framework (system-level), modular components: KV-cache manager,
  decoder, scheduler.
- Throughput **1,100 TPS on HumanEval, average 800 TPS** across 6 benchmarks
  at batch 1 on 8×H800.
- **10× speedup over Fast-dLLM** with similar performance.
- 2-3× over QWen2.5-3B with vLLM.

## Method

System framework integrating multiple methods. "Vicinity KV-cache refresh"
selectively updates a small window of tokens **adjacent to the current
decoding block**. Geometry-based (not learned/drift-based).

## Why it matters here

**Engineering benchmark we'd have to beat (or compose with).** Their gains
are largely from system-level pipelining + kernel optimization, plus a
simple vicinity-window refresh. Our per-(layer × position) refresh-only is
a different axis.

The "vicinity refresh" is geometric (block-adjacent), not per-(layer ×
position) and not drift-aware. Likely composable with our per-position
refresh: dInfer chooses WHICH BLOCK area to refresh; we'd choose WHICH
POSITIONS in those layers.

## Open questions raised

- Is dInfer's "vicinity" the same width across all layers? (Likely yes,
  given the language.) If so, layer-stratified refresh stacks orthogonally.
- Their kernel implementation could be a baseline / engineering reference
  for our Phase 14b.
